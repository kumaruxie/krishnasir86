You Know the Answer. So Why Does Your Mouth Stop?

Has this ever happened to you? Someone asks you a question in English. The answer is ready in your mind. But when it is time to speak, nothing comes out. So you smile, say "yes, okay", and move on.

Then you reach home and think: "I should have said this."

You studied English for 10 to 12 years. Grammar, tenses, essays, all of it. Still, when the call comes, your mind goes blank.

Here is the truth: this is not your fault. You do know English. Nobody ever taught you the grammar that is used for speaking.

And this silence has a cost. It is the opportunity that went to someone who spoke up in the meeting. It is the call you passed to a junior. It is the interview you prepared for and still lost.

Grammar Is of Three Kinds. Only One Helps You Speak.

School teaches reading and writing. Speaking is almost never taught. It is like practising batting for years and expecting to become a bowler.

Type of Grammar Where You Learn It Helps You Speak?
School grammar (academic) School and college books No, it is for reading and writing
Exam grammar (competitive) SSC, bank, board exam prep No, it is for written English
Speaking grammar (communicative) Daily-life sentence patterns Yes, it is made for speaking

Most people only ever learn the first two. That is why you know the grammar and still cannot speak.

Meet the CLT Method

CLT stands for Communicative Language Teaching. You learn English the way you learned Hindi, by speaking it in real situations, not by studying rules. When you speak Hindi, you never stop to think about grammar. CLT builds the same habit in English.

See It in 10 Seconds: Take One Pattern — "I want to..."

Tap any phrase below to see how one pattern gives you multiple sentences without memorising:

I want to go home.
"I want to go home."
"I want to talk to my boss."
"I want to learn English."

One pattern, three sentences, no thinking. That is speaking grammar. Not a rule to memorise, a pattern to use.

How Krishna Gaur's 3-Level System Works

Level 1 — Sentence Building

Learn speaking grammar and bring your "Hindi to English" thinking time down from minutes to seconds.

Level 2 — Learning in a Community

Once you can speak basic sentences, you practise in a group with people just like you, who have the same hesitation and make the same mistakes. You are never alone, and the fear goes faster.

Level 3 — Professional Skills

Meetings, interviews, presentations and discussions. Basics first, then the rest. Nothing stays standing on a weak base.
